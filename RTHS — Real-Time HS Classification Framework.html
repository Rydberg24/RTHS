01 — The gap

Every WMS classifies after storage. That's the whole problem.

Put-away decisions are made on dimensions, weight, and pick frequency — never on tariff exposure. Classification happens later, manually, at export. Industry estimates put 20–40% of large-retail customs delays down to avoidable errors made in that gap, and misclassification penalties in the EU can exceed €50,000 per shipment.

The legacy workflow

  • 24–48 hour staging holds. Pallets sit idle awaiting manual broker review before any export filing can begin.
  • Storage decided blind. High-duty and low-duty goods are shelved side by side with no tariff-risk awareness.
  • Errors surface late. Reverse logistics kicks in only once goods are already deep in the supply chain.
Baseline hold, all items
48.0 hrs

The RTHS workflow

  • Classified at the scan. ATLAS returns an HS code and a confidence score in well under 0.5 seconds, asynchronously.
  • Risk, not guesswork. Confidence is combined with duty exposure, penalty severity, volume and expiry into one score.
  • Routed automatically. Three tiers — GREEN, YELLOW, RED — tell the WMS exactly where to send the pallet.

02 — Why it works

Grounded in three literatures, not just one product idea.

Decision theory under uncertainty

Von Neumann & Morgenstern's expected-utility logic treats put-away as a choice under uncertainty. ATLAS's confidence score stands in for a probability estimate; the risk formula scales the response to both the odds and the cost of being wrong.

Information processing theory

Galbraith frames the 24–48 hour delay as a capacity mismatch: human customs teams can't process classification requests at inbound scale. RTHS substitutes machine processing at the exact point that mismatch occurs.

Industry 4.0 / cyber-physical systems

A dock that classifies goods automatically and routes them by an AI-generated score is a cyber-physical system — a physical process augmented by a digital decision layer, per Kagermann and Lee.

parallelism

Scanning never waits on the model — classification runs asynchronously in a background thread.

progressive commitment

Low-risk items commit to storage immediately; only uncertain cases are held for review.

risk proportionality

Scrutiny scales with exposure — a multi-factor score, never a single pass/fail accuracy threshold.

graceful degradation

Every scan is persisted regardless of classifier uptime, so nothing is lost if ATLAS is unreachable.

07 — Scope

What this is, and what it isn't yet.

This is a proof-of-concept simulation, not a validated production result. Treating it otherwise would misrepresent the research — so here's what's still open.

Synthetic dataset

10,000 records were generated, not captured from a live receiving dock. Empirical validation on real scan data is the next step.

Mock classifier

Confidence scores come from a heuristic stand-in for ATLAS, not the live model's calibrated token-level output.

No ground-truth check

HS codes weren't verified against real customs rulings — the 45% figure is a modelled estimate, not a measured one.

Single product mix, U.S. HTS

Configured for one generalized 8-category mix under U.S. Harmonised Tariff Schedule rules; other jurisdictions need localisation.

RTHS is a decision-support layer, not a replacement for a licensed customs broker — consistent with CBP ruling HQ H350722, which holds that the broker retains responsibility for entry classification regardless of which tool assisted the screening.
